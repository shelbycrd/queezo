## Editing and sharing

A deck belongs to its creator and begins private. The deck router checks ownership before mutation and applies account, deck, and card limits at the server. A flashcard has a Front and Back, with optional raster photo data on each side. The editor exposes 250 characters per side and paginates saved cards by 20. The backend schema supports legacy text through 10,000 characters, restricts image format and size, and rejects link-like content in editable deck and card text. Creation makes no Ollama call. Public decks populate the community feed, and public and unlisted decks support token-based viewing, direct study, and copying for authenticated users. Copying creates new private ownership and card IDs. Privacy revocation changes the share grant. ## Classic study

Owned sessions snapshot cards and persist a randomized initial order, presentation token, current card, review history, and scheduling state. Reveal and rate operations validate the active presentation. The scheduler uses Hard, Normal, and Easy to decide whether and when a card returns. A rating records activity and advances or completes the session. The browser orchestrates optional timing and keyboard controls. Direct Classic study of a shared deck uses the browser and creates no server-side review history. The deck's previous-session surface shows the latest eligible owned result across modes. ## Solo Multiple-Choice

The backend authorizes the deck, shuffles the card IDs once, persists one position per card, and submits a bounded preparation job. A shared session is bound to its initiator and the current share-grant digest. The preparer queries caches in batches and can materialize cached positions early. It processes uncached positions in sequence. While preparation runs, early play requires up to five ready questions in the resolved opening sequence. A permanent failure is skipped. A pending position stops the readiness scan, so a later cache hit cannot move ahead of it. The frontend polls preparation, automatically starts when the opening threshold is reached, and waits for a pending next position. A terminal failure can be bypassed without reordering the remaining playable cards. [Session API](../../backend/app/routers/multiple_choice.py), [preparation and grading](../../backend/app/services/multiple_choice.py), [study UI](../../frontend/src/views/MultipleChoiceView.vue)

An opened question exposes opaque option IDs and text. It stores shuffled choices, the original Back as the correct answer, and an authoritative deadline when applicable. Submission validates the session, question, current position, and selected option. The first owned presentation to a learner, and each question in a direct shared session, can retain one wrong choice and award a second try. The eliminated choice and attempt state survive refresh. The finalized submission returns feedback and advances progress. Completion requires preparation to settle and all available questions to be resolved, unless the deck timer expires. Preparing, failed, active, completed, and cancelled are persisted states. Retry, continue with eligible questions, expiration, and cancellation have separate guards. Owned results can contribute to history and activity. Shared attempts are excluded. [Session service](../../backend/app/services/multiple_choice.py), [question persistence](../../backend/app/models.py)

## Inference and shared cache

1. Read the card under its owning deck. Compare the cached Front/Back fingerprint, generation version, candidate validity, and fallback provenance. A model mismatch invalidates AI-dependent results. Pure deck fallback is independent of the configured model.
2. On a hit, use the same three distractors for any authorized solo or room consumer. The shuffled choices and option IDs belong to the session snapshot.
3. On a miss, serialize local inference and obtain a short, fenced SQLite job lease for this card. Recheck the cache before claiming. Waiters can see the winning row and reuse it. No database write transaction spans inference.
4. The generator calls Ollama `POST /api/chat` in JSON output mode with thinking disabled. It initially asks for six candidates and adapts its prompt across at most three attempts. Checks reject malformed JSON and values, normalized equality with the original answer, normalized duplicates, and additional invalid patterns. HTTPX disables ambient proxies and redirects. Partial candidates are retained on recoverable failure.
5. Fill any missing slots from distinct Back values in the same deck. The save transaction rechecks the card fingerprint, ownership, and lease, validates the final choices, and persists all three with provenance. An insufficient result becomes a preparation failure with no valid cache row. Lease expiry permits recovery after a dead worker.
6. Card Front/Back edits or source removal invalidate affected entries. Fallback dependencies identify affected cached cards. An already prepared question remains a snapshot with its established choices and correct option.
